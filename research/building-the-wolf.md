# Building the Wolf — research haul

Source material for the "Raised by Wolves" essay: the empirical case that adversity
and outsider status, *survived and come through*, become long-run advantages. Sorted
by how much weight each finding will bear. Built to be debunk-proof — a piece arguing
"hardship is an edge" is exactly what a hostile reader wants to catch overclaiming.

## The through-line

Across every theme, one structure recurs, and it is the "wrong one in the wrong place"
thesis, proven: **the advantage lives in the moderate / recovered-from / trait-level
version, and it is a specialization tuned to a volatile environment — which reads as a
gift or a deficit depending on the room.** It's not the wound that strengthens. It's
the constitution that produced a *survivable* version of it, the survival, and the
better soil found afterward.

## Tier 1 — The spine (build here)

1. **The U-curve of adversity.** *Moderate* lifetime adversity beats both *none* and
   *high*: lower distress, more resilience, least rattled by new blows — shown in the
   body (pain tolerance, "challenge" cardiovascular response), not just self-report.
   - Seery, Holman & Silver, *JPSP* 2010 (n≈2,000, longitudinal): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20939649/
   - Lab follow-up, *Psychological Science* 2013: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23673992/

2. **Differential susceptibility / vantage sensitivity.** The same wiring that makes you
   break harder in bad soil makes you bloom harder in good — "for better and for worse."
   Intervention data: highly sensitive kids benefited far more from a prevention program.
   - Lionetti orchids/tulips/dandelions, *Translational Psychiatry* 2018: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-017-0090-6
   - Pluess, vantage sensitivity: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5959990/
   - Caveat: drop the old "one orchid gene" (5-HTTLPR) story — it failed to replicate (Border et al., *AJP* 2019: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548317/). Treat "highly sensitive person" as a partly-neuroticism-laden pop label, not a clean category (Greven 2019 review: https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1827&context=psy-research).

3. **Hidden talents.** Harsh/unpredictable upbringing → *worse* inhibition but *better*
   attention-shifting and threat detection — **and the advantage appears only when tested
   under uncertainty**, the conditions the mind is adapted to. The specialization is
   invisible in calm rooms and decisive in volatile ones.
   - Mittal et al., *JPSP* 2015: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26414842/
   - Frankenhuis & Nettle 2020 (self-critical framework): https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0963721419881154

## Tier 2 — Strong specific pegs

4. **Defensive pessimism.** Anxious people who set low expectations and mentally rehearse
   what could go wrong prepare harder and match optimists' performance; force them to
   "relax" and they do *worse*. The cleanest "anxiety as fuel" evidence.
   - Norem & Cantor: https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2007.00053.x

5. **Multicultural experience → creativity** (depth-of-adaptation is the moderator, not
   mere travel); **multilingual *exposure* → perspective-taking** (76% vs 50% chance —
   it's social practice at tracking who-means-what, not a brain upgrade).
   - Maddux & Galinsky, *JPSP* 2009: https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp9651047.pdf
   - Fan/Kinzler, *Psychological Science* 2015: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4791950/

6. **Immigrants found firms at ~2× the native rate** (census-scale) — outsiders build.
   Caveat: selection + blocked-mobility, not proof biculturalism did it.
   - Azoulay et al., NBER w27778: https://www.nber.org/papers/w27778

7. **Plainness beats the "beauty premium."** The beauty advantage is a *bias in the
   observer, not real competence* — looks don't predict actual leadership quality — and
   attractive people get trusted *less* and penalized for disappointing the expectations
   their face sets. So the plain one is judged on substance and over-delivers.
   - Texas A&M 2025 (beauty bias in leader perception): https://stories.tamu.edu/news/2025/11/26/beauty-bias-strongly-shapes-who-we-view-as-leaders/
   - Wilson & Eckel, trust games: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245896687_Detecting_Trustworthiness_Does_Beauty_Confound_Intuition
   - Vivid but complicating: dictators have colder, less attractive faces than elected leaders (Giacomin et al., *SPPS* 2021): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8358563/

8. **Scarcity's "focus dividend."** The poor judge prices and opportunity-costs more
   accurately; the *same* tunneling that focuses also neglects everything outside it —
   the gift and the curse are one mechanism.
   - Mullainathan & Shafir, *Scarcity* (2013).

9. **Childhood adversity → elevated adult empathy.**
   - Greenberg et al., *PLOS ONE* 2018: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0203886

10. **The underdog effect.** Being doubted raises performance — *only when the doubters
    lack credibility*; when the low expectations are credible, they backfire.
    - Nurmohamed, *AMJ* 2020: https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amj.2017.0181

11. **Anxiety's other upsides** (moderate, harnessed — not clinical): faster threat
    detection/vigilance; enhanced error-monitoring; worry correlates with *verbal* IQ
    (the same engine that simulates futures in detail produces both foresight and worry).

## Guardrails (cite these and you're bulletproof)

- **Dose matters — the hard floor.** 4+ Adverse Childhood Experiences → sharply worse
  health, ~20-year life-expectancy hit. Severe/chronic/childhood trauma is damage, not
  tuition. The benefit is confined to *moderate, buffered, survived* adversity.
- **Bullying is net-harmful on average** (Copeland, *JAMA Psychiatry* 2013: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3618584/; Wolke/Lereya, *Lancet Psychiatry* 2015: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4580734/). Strengths show up in a minority only — weakest peg, don't lean on it. (And note: in the data, the group with *no* elevated anxiety/depression was the *bullies*, not the victims — don't accidentally valorize the aggressor.)
- **Most self-reported "post-traumatic growth" is illusory** — it doesn't track measured
  change and coexists with *more* distress (Frazier, *Psychological Science* 2009: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02381.x; Boals 2023). Deploy this *as* the honesty move — it pre-empts the toxic-positivity objection.

## Do NOT cite (these get you debunked)

- **Depressive realism** ("sadder but wiser") — 2022 preregistered null; pooled effect ~d=0.07. Dead. (Dev et al.: https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/8/1/38529/194062)
- **"Depression makes you more creative"** — meta-analysis says weak/mixed; the real signal is the *bipolar spectrum in unaffected relatives*, not depression, and full illness *impairs* creativity. Glamorizing it is the one move that would genuinely embarrass you. (Taylor meta-analysis 2017: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1745691617699653)
- **The analytical-rumination hypothesis** ("depression is adaptive problem-solving") — provocative but untested and edges toward "don't treat it." Label as a debated hypothesis if used at all.
- **General "bilingual brains are sharper" (executive function)** — deflated under replication (Dick et al., ABCD n≈4,500: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7156280/). The durable bilingual finding is narrow and social (perspective-taking, #5).
- **Grit as a superpower** — r≈.15, basically repackaged conscientiousness (Credé et al. 2017).
- **"Poverty = −13 IQ points" (Mani 2013)** — contested, didn't cleanly replicate. Cite as "influential but contested" if at all.
