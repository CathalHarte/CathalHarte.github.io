# Dog Cognition — research haul

The foil to the wild wolf: the bred, domesticated dog. Dog-vs-wolf behavioural
science, anchored to primary papers. Throughout, which findings **controlled for
rearing** (wolves and dogs raised identically — the gold standard, Vienna Wolf
Science Center) versus older confounded pet-dog-vs-wild-wolf designs.

## The centerpiece — "the dog looks back; the wolf keeps working"

- **Miklósi, Kubinyi, Topál, Gácsi, Virányi & Csányi (2003), "A simple reason for a
  big difference: wolves do not look back at humans, but dogs do," *Current Biology*
  13:763–766.** The title image of the essay. Verbatim: *"after undergoing training
  to solve a simple manipulation task, dogs that are faced with an insoluble version
  of the same problem look/gaze at the human, while socialized wolves do not."* They
  add this readiness "cannot be achieved in wolves even after extended socialization."
  Wolves and dogs were "socialized to humans at comparable levels."
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12725735/ · open copy: https://www.wellbeingintlstudiesrepository.org/comski/12/

- **⭐ The gem that closes the loophole — Rao, Range, Kadletz, Kotrschal &
  Marshall-Pescini (2018), "Differences in persistence between dogs and wolves in an
  unsolvable task in the absence of humans," *PeerJ* 6:e5944.** With NO human in the
  room, *"Wolves were more persistent than dogs… in their manipulation of the objects."*
  Gold-standard identical rearing (12 wolves, 17 dogs, all hand-raised with conspecifics).
  This proves the dog's look-back is a **chosen strategy — offload the problem onto the
  master — not mere incompetence.** The wolf owns the problem; the dog outsources it.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6266929/

- Companion: Topál et al. (2005), "Attachment to humans," *Animal Behaviour* 70:1367–1375
  — dogs treat a human caregiver as a secure base (Strange Situation Test). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222419926

## Dogs read humans precociously (the domestication hypothesis of social cognition)

- **Hare, Brown, Williamson & Tomasello (2002), "The domestication of social cognition
  in dogs," *Science* 298:1634–1636.** Following a human point/gaze to hidden food, dogs
  beat chimpanzees *and* wolves; even low-contact puppies had it. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12446914/ · PDF: https://evolutionaryanthropology.duke.edu/sites/evolutionaryanthropology.duke.edu/files/site-images/Hareetal2002.pdf
- **Myth to avoid:** the strong claim "wolves *can't* read human cues" is false — properly
  socialized, identically-raised wolves can; dogs are just more *effortlessly and
  precociously* attentive. (Range/Virányi correction.)

## Neoteny — the dog is a wolf that never grows up

- **Belyaev–Trut silver-fox experiment (Trut 1999, *American Scientist* 87:160; Dugatkin
  & Trut 2018 review).** Selecting foxes for **tameness alone** produced, within ~6
  generations, animals that licked handlers and wagged their tails — and dragged along a
  "domestication syndrome": floppy ears, curled tails, white mottling, shorter juvenile
  snouts, lowered stress hormones. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12052-018-0090-x
- **⭐ Gem:** breeding for tameness = breeding for *permanent puppyhood*. Domestication
  keeps the juvenile and loses the self-sufficient adult — the mechanistic parallel to the
  dog's dependence and "look back at the master." The bred one is arrested; it never has
  to grow out of needing its parent.
- **Myth to avoid:** the clean "single tameness gene, from truly wild foxes" story is
  contested (Lord et al. 2020). Use the fox experiment as vivid illustration, not settled
  mechanism.

## Where the wolf BEATS the dog (identical-rearing, the honest correction)

- **Range & Virányi (2015), "Canine Cooperation Hypothesis," *Frontiers in Psychology*
  5:1582.** Dogs and wolves "raised and kept in an identical way." Dog–human cooperation
  was *built on* wolf social skills, not created from scratch. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4295532/
- **⭐ The tolerance flip:** *"wolves co-fed longer than the dogs"* and *"dogs form a
  steeper dominance hierarchy than wolves."* The wild wolf *shares* food and holds a
  flatter hierarchy with packmates; the dog monopolises by rank. This undercuts the lazy
  "savage wolf / gentle dog" trope and deepens the irony — the domesticated one is the
  more despotic among its own kind.
- **Independent problem-solving:** watching a demonstrator open a puzzle box, "all wolves"
  succeeded vs only 4 of 15 dogs. **Causal reasoning:** wolves solve the string/cups
  causal task above chance; dogs at chance. Wolves attend to cause-and-effect the dog
  ignores.
- Nuance: "Wolves lead and dogs follow, but both cooperate with humans" (Range et al.
  2019, *Sci Reports*) — the dog isn't a worse cooperator, it defaults to a *deferent*
  role. And hand-raised wolves *do* form attachment-like bonds (Hansen Wheat 2022). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-40468-y

## The domestication bargain

- **Self-domestication (Hare & Woods, *Survival of the Friendliest*, 2020).** The
  friendliest wolves domesticated *themselves* — tolerating humans bought reliable
  scavenged food and safety; friendliness dragged the dog phenotype along. A mutualism,
  not a human plan. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/how-wolves-really-became-dogs-180970014/
- **The trade, for the essay:** the dog exchanged the wolf's independent problem-solving,
  causal reasoning, and self-reliance for social attentiveness to humans and a secure
  partnership — the "look back at the master" strategy. Neoteny is *how* the trade is
  written into the body: keep the pup, lose the self-sufficient adult. Guaranteed food
  and safety, in exchange for autonomy — the exact inverse of the disperser's gamble.
- Leading hypothesis, not proven fact — where/when/how-many-times dogs were domesticated
  is genuinely unsettled.

## Metaphor gems, ranked

1. **Miklósi 2003** — the unsolvable jar: the dog turns its face to the human; the
   socialized wolf keeps wrestling the lid. The literal title image.
2. **Rao/Range 2018** — with no human in the room, the wolf is *more persistent*. The
   dog's looking-back is a *strategy* (offload to the master), not incompetence.
3. **Belyaev–Trut foxes** — breed for tameness and you breed for permanent puppyhood;
   dependence rides along for free. The dog is the wolf that never grew up.
4. **The tolerance flip** — the wild wolf shares food and keeps a flatter hierarchy with
   its own; the dog monopolises by rank. The domesticated one is the more despotic.

## Accuracy caveat to state once in the essay

The earliest, most-cited "dog beats wolf" results (Hare 2002; parts of Miklósi 2003)
compared pet dogs with less-comparably-raised wolves. The Vienna Wolf Science Center's
identical-rearing studies are what separate *domestication* from *upbringing* — and they
show the picture is a **trade, not dog superiority**: the dog gained the human, the wolf
kept the problem.
