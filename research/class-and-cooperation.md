# Social Class & Cooperation — research haul

The human version of the wolf/dog "tolerance flip": does having less make you share
more and read others better, while wealth breeds self-focus? A famous literature says
yes — and the best large-scale and preregistered replications say **not so fast.**
Use this as a *lens, not a law.*

## The headline honesty flag (read first)

The "the poor are kinder / more cooperative" story is **real as published findings but
genuinely contested, and the strongest tests contradict it.** The original effects come
mostly from one Berkeley lab (Keltner's) using lab games and small field studies; the
large representative-sample and preregistered replications that followed **mostly failed
to reproduce them, and several found the opposite.** Safe to use the *theory* and the
*imagery*; not safe to assert "the poor are more generous" as fact.

## The famous findings (evocative — attribute, don't assert)

- **Having less, giving more.** Piff, Kraus, Côté, Cheng & Keltner (2010), *JPSP* —
  lower-class individuals gave more in a dictator game, were more trusting/generous, more
  helpful to a distressed other. Foundational, hugely cited, effects modest — and this is
  the paper the replications targeted and missed. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20649364/
- **Higher class → more unethical.** Piff et al. (2012), *PNAS* (seven studies):
  higher-status cars ~4× more likely to cut off other drivers and to fail to yield to a
  pedestrian; people made to *feel* higher-class took ~twice as much candy from a jar
  reserved for children; more lying in negotiation, more cheating for a prize. Mediator:
  attitudes toward greed. Memorable, but the field studies are small and not
  well-powered-replicated. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1118373109
- **⭐ The rigged Monopoly** (Piff TED talk): a coin flip randomly made one player rich
  (double money/salary/dice). Rich players got louder, ruder, ate more from the shared
  pretzel bowl — and when asked why they'd won, **credited their own skill, not the rigged
  advantage.** A TED/classroom demo, *not* a controlled study — lean on the image, flag
  the status. Published cousin: Piff (2014), "Wealth and the Inflated Self: Class,
  Entitlement, and Narcissism," *PSPB*. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0146167213501699
- **Lower class read others better.** Kraus, Côté & Keltner (2010), *Psych Science* —
  higher empathic accuracy (reading emotions, eyes-test). Small samples, one lab. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20974714/
- **Lower class report more compassion** (Stellar et al. 2012, *Emotion*), with heart-rate
  orienting to a suffering other. Same caveats. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22148992/

## ⭐ The safe ground — the theory, not the contested experiments

- **Solipsism vs. contextualism.** Kraus, Piff, Mendoza-Denton, Rheinschmidt & Keltner
  (2012), *Psychological Review* — scarcity and low rank push toward **contextualism**
  (attention to external forces and other people); abundance and high rank push toward
  **solipsism** (focus on one's own states and goals). This is the framework, stated as
  theory, so quoting it commits you to less. **The cleanest defensible metaphor: the one
  who has less must read the room; the one who has more can afford not to.** https://krauslab.com/SESpsychreview.2012.pdf
- **Keltner's *Power Paradox* (2016)** ties it all together and is the *safest* frame,
  because it's about what power does to *anyone*, not a fixed trait of a class: people
  rise by empathy and cooperation, and then gaining power/status **erodes the very
  capacities that earned it** — more impulsive, less empathic, worse at reading others,
  more entitled. This also links straight to the essay's existing "power cannot be taken"
  (Arendt) thread.

## ⚠️ The counterweight — the replication fight (give it real weight)

- **Korndörfer, Egloff & Schmukle (2015), *PLOS ONE*** — eight studies on large
  nationally representative datasets (German SOEP, US CEX/GSS, ISSP across 30 countries;
  n ≈ 4,000–83,000). Higher-class people were *more* likely to donate, gave a higher
  *share* of income, and volunteered more. Their bottom line: *"under no condition did we
  find the negative effect… reported in the psychological literature."* https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4507988/
- **Stamos et al. (2020), *J. Research in Personality*** — two high-powered *preregistered*
  replications of Piff's giving paradigm. No link: overall effect **r ≈ .01**; everyone
  shared ~30% regardless of wealth. The killer citation for "don't overclaim." https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656619301230
- The follow-up "inequality is the moderator" rescue hypothesis **also** failed a
  large-scale test. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0220723
- The field's own society (SPSP) declines to declare a winner and urges caution. https://spsp.org/news-center/character-context-blog/are-poor-people-actually-more-generous

## The reconciliation — where both can be true (the essay's best home)

- **Absolute vs. proportional giving:** the wealthy give more total dollars and volunteer
  more; the sympathetic reading is that the poor give a larger *share* of what little they
  have — but even that is contested. Frame as the kinder reading, not a proven fact.
- **Interpersonal/immediate vs. institutional/aggregate:** the Keltner effects show up in
  face-to-face, communal, in-the-moment settings (helping a distressed person, reading a
  face); the contradicting effects come from formal donations and organized volunteering.
  It's coherent that **scarcity sharpens immediate mutual aid while affluence enables
  formal institutional giving** — different currencies of prosociality. This is the
  honest reconciliation, because it concedes the counter-evidence.
- **Felt vs. objective class:** effects are often stronger for *primed* rank than for real
  income — supporting the "power changes the self" reading, but also implying the lab
  effects may be momentary mindset, not durable class character.

## Bottom line for staying bulletproof

- **Safe to assert:** the theory (solipsism vs contextualism; the power paradox); that
  striking demonstrations exist; that the wealthy give more in absolute terms.
- **Must hedge/attribute:** "the poor are kinder/more cooperative." Say "in Keltner's lab
  studies…" and immediately add "though large representative-sample and preregistered
  replications have largely failed to reproduce this."
- **Best essay tie-in:** the rigged-Monopoly winner **crediting his own genius for a
  coin-flip advantage** is your bred dog exactly — given the double-money start ("told a
  thousand times he's destined to be great"), then sure he earned it. Use it as
  illustration, flag it as a demo.
- **The one honest sentence:** *the idea that having less makes you share more is a
  beautiful and partly-evidenced hypothesis that the best large-scale tests have not
  confirmed — so use it as a lens, not a law.*
