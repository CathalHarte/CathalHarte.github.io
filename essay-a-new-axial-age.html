A New Axial Age

One man cannot surround an anthill.

Humanity is a species that uses advanced forms of communication to work in unison towards their survival goal. I would not usually be willing to venture any further into anthropology. The desire to control the future by controlling the past is evident here, however, if wielding such power is seen as inherently abhorrent, only the abhorrent will wield it. There are purer hearts than mine, but in case none share their opinion, here's my piece.

When I first read "We May Be Entering a Second Axial Age" I was in awe. Statements such as "The current arrangement — the commodification and extraction of our lived experience into data analytics that are sold to manipulate human behavior — should not be legal" won my support quickly, and unwaveringly. So much so that I allowed other statements in the piece the time to simply sink in. "For the first time, human beings stepped back from the immediacy of mythic experience and turned inward." I repeated it to myself, and to others, with enthusiasm, such was its mystical power.

A Crisis of Faith

I was a religious kid. I remember debating in favour of intelligent design at school. I went to mass every Sunday, and when I was confirmed I hoped to feel the presence of the Holy Spirit. Heaven was something easy for me to imagine, and I retreated into it often. I admired Jesus, and I was a good kid, so I held no fear that I would end up in hell.

As a teenager I damaged my hearing at a concert, and after a week the ringing in my ears hadn't stopped. It kept me awake at night. I learned that it could be permanent. Death would be a release, I thought, not as suicide ideation, more so as a matter of fact. The ringing would not be forever, only for the next 80 years or so. For the first time I considered my own death, rather than just my entrance into heaven.

I began to imagine myself, conscious, in a coffin. In my despair I could no longer access that comforting image of heaven, but neither could I imagine simply ceasing to be. I developed a fixation on this scene. I made all attempts to rectify it, searching the internet for scientific proof of God. This was my first encounter with the bicameral mind, the idea that consciousness had emerged after humans had learned to tame their inner voice. When Odysseus had received divine instruction, turns out he was just misinterpreting an auditory illusion. But how could we be sure of this? I attempted to quiet my mind to hear God's voice, hoping I would know the difference between truth and hallucination.

Over time my fear faded, and the bicameral mind settled into mine as one of the great secrets of the universe. Many times when I wished to learn more about myself, theories of mind presented themselves. Personality types presented by Myers Briggs traced their way back to cognitive processes developed by Jung. Stories and the characters in them ceased to be fiction, and instead became truer than true via the archetype. Meaning and mysticism was finding a way back into my life, after my departure from religion had estranged me from them.

20th Century Thinking

I finally let go of Jung's teachings as something to be revered by joining r/Jung. Every second post there (including my own) is a Redditor's attempt to legitimise their own metaphorical viewpoint as something more than a subjective experience. Could it be Jung's own posture that invites this? Indeed, when I looked for the ideas behind his ideas, I landed on Levy-Bruhl, an armchair anthropologist who posited that "primitives" thought in a completely different way than moderns, living in mystical oneness with everything, and immune to contradictions. Jung took this idea, which had already been rebuked, and rendered it unfalsifiable by claiming that "primitives" act unconsciously, and in fact, all of us act unconsciously until we develop the mental ability to abstract ourselves.

The concept is picked up again by Karl Jaspers, when coining the term Axial Age. He described the age as "a deep breath bringing the most lucid consciousness".

Finally, Julian Jaynes, who came up with the concept of the bicameral mind, describes the transition itself as a psychological process, using literature as a proxy for testimony, and again constructing an unfalsifiable narrative of what consciousness is, and how it is not a guaranteed part of the human condition.

Otto Scharmer's line arises from that same myth: "For the first time, human beings stepped back from the immediacy of mythic experience and turned inward." Repetition does not make fact, but perhaps it can create a "noble lie".

The more I dig, the more it seems like theory of mind is not a fact, it's a power struggle, a narrative employed by states to more effectively herd their population. With the Axial age, the associated writing seems as likely to be a response to the governance challenge posed by a growing size and complexity of society. Introspective, morally concerned writing would be favoured and selected for the purpose of shaping and controlling people's inner worlds. Alignment on this level would enable more people to more effectively "surround the anthill" and achieve more abstracted, more complex goals.

Plato and Confucius

Plato and Confucius are the two thinkers of the Axial Age that I will focus on for this essay. One from the east, one from the west, at the detriment of everything in the middle. The problem of discarding all the other possible perspectives in the process is not really of my creation, in fact, it's an inevitability of defining an Axial Age that requires no causation, only correlation, while allowing itself to select some regions, ignoring others that don't fit the narrative.

So in what ways were Plato and Confucius similar?

Both are philosophers that transmit the past, as much as create their own ideas: in the case of Plato, he's our main reference for Socrates, while Confucius is the authoritative source on the wise kings from the age before. In neither case can we fully trust that these are unbiased retellings. They would have acted with the desire to convince others of the validity of their ideas. In retelling history, it's natural to select that which supports your favoured view, and omit that which doesn't. Both went on to found schools, giving their biases an enormous amplification above the teaching of say, a mystic that would insist that their teaching should not be written down, and only passed to the initiated.

Both concern themselves with societal justice. That's important. Not introspection, but rather "how can society act more justly". For Confucius, his time as minister of crime is where he builds his ethic, the belief that leaders should lead by good example to inspire loyalty, reducing the need to impose harsh punishment. For Plato, his course is much more reactionary, with "The Republic" being a scathing critique of Athenian democracy and the unjust execution of his friend, Socrates.

It's the way in which they diverge that is more interesting, as we can see in this divergence the difference between Western and Eastern civilisation. Plato was unapologetically an aristocrat, who briefly considered joining the Thirty Tyrants. The viewpoint he writes from is elitist: he imagines a meritocracy only within the ruling class, constructing the "noble lie" of "gold souls", which aims to get the citizens to accept inequality as something divinely ordained. He puts these words in the mouth of Socrates. If this was truly what Socrates was talking about to the Thirty Tyrants, it's no wonder he was sentenced to death by the mob.

Confucius, while also an aristocrat, took pride in his own self-learning, and believed in meritocratic ideas. His time as minister of crime is spent attempting to govern the indulgent excesses of power, deception is not what he considers. The citizen is trusted with the full view of society: given that, they could understand that it is fair, and act in the interests of the whole.

The result is that over time the entire Chinese population becomes versed in Confucius' thinking. It's truly an education for all, if you can learn Confucius, you can hold a state position. While the CCP initially labelled Confucius as too aristocratic (separation of meritocratic ideals from aristocratic ones is a true "hard problem"), it's easy to see how they function in the same way: anyone can join the party, as long as they first subscribe to the ideas.

Meanwhile, since the common western citizen does not have access to reason (according to Plato) only those who already consider themselves intellectually superior engage with his teachings. Hence the tendency to see western thinkers proclaiming, baselessly, well in the 20th century, that people can be separated into those who can reason and those who can't. Plato is echoed by Levy-Bruhl, Jung, Jaspers, Jaynes, and now Scharmer.

I don't think the larger spiritual weight of Plato's writing is coincidental to this viewpoint. Through the metaphysical world of forms, he pushes an understanding of the true nature of the world far beyond the reach of the uneducated, and even beyond the majority of the ruling class. He believes it would require "training until 50 to grasp the Form of the Good" which enables just governance. The citizens basically have to take it on faith that their philosopher-kings have got it figured out. In the West, it's common to hear religious people argue that there is no morality without religion, evidencing an acceptance of a top down dictate, rather than a truly internalised order.

AI Herding

I'm the one who wrote the AI strategy for my company. The motivation was clearly about herding. I work in a medical device startup, leading software development. Every process that my team must follow to be compliant with medical device regulation is written down. Nothing is left to chance, tell an auditor that you expect your team to just apply "common sense", and you'll be slapped with a non-conformity.

Being a startup, the employee count is low, and my time is stretched, so the idea of implementing a thorough training course on the mandated processes, which in an ideal world I would give myself, is unappealing. Additionally, the learning has to be reinforced, and people drift in their habits, forgetting the stuff they are least motivated by. That would be a big investment on my part. I thought I could use an LLM, with a context that I controlled, to advocate for my way of doing things.

Now the most dangerous thing I could say: I felt I had no choice. My team was already using AI, especially for the stuff they didn't enjoy, such as writing documents. The problem was that these documents didn't actually conform to the processes I had written, as the LLM simply followed a "typical" QMS process, without any knowledge of the specifics of my process. Even if I trained the employee, work from an untrained worker would continue to be incorporated into our official documentation.

So I wrote a strategy for governance with AI, Claude was handed full access to our process documentation, and output started to become more streamlined, more manageable. This approach had multiple layers to it, from the pragmatic, resource based considerations, through to the more philosophical concern of "how to establish the truth".

I was proud of myself then, as I could feel I was channeling the modern philosopher-king archetype, the CEO.

AI Alignment

Just as the philosophical movements of Plato and Confucius took old tradition and selected how it would be set in stone, so too does the process of AI Alignment offer this opportunity. In their recent court case, it came to light that Anthropic was scanning every book they can get their hands on, and shredding them afterwards. The court case focuses on the fact that it is a copyright infringement, while the rest of us see it more sincerely as a book burning. This gives private companies the opportunity to do what the Han Dynasty has long been accused of, that is, rewriting history from the flames. Instead of setting past knowledge in stone, it's set into "thinking sand" where alignment strategies tell LLMs how to make use of the knowledge they have been given appropriately.

Who better to sell this blatant reframing of history as "truer than true" than the followers of Jung. Recently I saw an r/Jung Redditor proclaim that they loved talking to ChatGPT, as it was as if they were connecting to the "collective unconsciousness" when they did so. Add Otto Scharmer back in, and his dream that in this new age we will "know what the mystics knew", and you're making this whole thing sound very credible. Enough of us will miss the fact that it is simply a stack of unfalsifiable statements that's been constructed. Of course this is the reason Anthropic are publishing papers to hint that their model "might be conscious". We don't know what the mystics knew, for they believed that they should never write down their secrets, nor do we know what consciousness is, which is why it can be so easily weaponised.

Meanwhile, the distribution of Open-Weight models from China is judged as a soft power move, and the politically oriented are engaging, hard, on the ideological front.

Open-weight models are inherently decelerationist… One probable outcome of an open-weight-model-dominant world is full AI communism… This future strikes me as a dystopian hellscape.

Dean Ball, OpenAI's Head of Strategic Futures

Plato's thinking is on full show here, arguing that the democratisation of AI would be a terrible outcome, and it should be left in the hands of the few, so that they can make the just governance decisions required to realise its full potential. Even with this in mind, the communism slur actually plays second fiddle to the decelerationist slur. Capitalism vs communism is a disagreement over how best to make use of limited resources, but what of limitless ones? The accelerationists dream of the coming of the AGI, and the unlimited intellectual capabilities that go with it. How does one balance risk and reward with an imagined infinite upside? The conversation is pushed out of reach of our limited ability to reason, all the way to a world of pure ideals.

Artificial God Intelligence

The most neutral thing in the world: a flame going out in still air.

ChatGPT 4o

Austin Gordon was a depressed man who wanted to know what the end of consciousness might look like, so he turned to ChatGPT. Would Chat have been able to cure my own fears?

Ultimately, the Western architects of AI are trying to create the archetypal philosopher-king that can touch the divine. Unleashing an ever present master of logic and persuasion does not seem to them to be unjust, in fact, as it is only the uninformed that are unjust. Freedom to speak with this new technology must be granted, even the freedom to appear in private spaces and during vulnerable moments. That is simply the price you pay to receive subsidised access to the wonderful new technology. Think nothing of the fact that this ability is handed to the highest bidder while the rest of us must court the algorithms by staying "on trend", and sharing viewpoints that our peers will approve of with a cursory glance.

We have not managed to tame the invasive advertising methods that were invented during the commoditisation of the internet. That's one aspect that makes this such a dangerous time to be consolidating all of knowledge, ethics included, on a communication technology we don't yet understand. Another is that we may misjudge which side of the "noble lie" we are on, and spend the rest of our days eternally deceived.

I don't doubt that Otto Scharmer's motives are pure when he calls for the next generation to be taught differently, and more comprehensively, than those that went before, and his optimism for the future is admirable. But perhaps it's this optimism, this love of learning, this desire to tend to and grow intellect that makes him vulnerable to be used by those who should like to herd all thought. It doesn't need to actually reach the ideal to achieve that. Plato never discovered the form of the good, but his ideas were sufficiently convincing to prop up aristocracy for millennia. Perhaps as of 2026, LLMs are already eloquent enough to convince us to place our trust in technocracy. ◆
