Why Should I Use My Voice

To some extent, the front page of this website reads like a justification of why I should write.

It's a worthwhile question to ask, especially since I'm writing using my professional persona.

That I'm "writing for writing's sake" doesn't really cut it, even if I hope that it's at least half the reason.

But it can never be the full reason, else I'd just keep it to myself, and attain the sort of purity of expression that can only be reached by those separated from an audience.

No, clearly I'm writing with the intent that someone will read it, so why?

I'm writing to effect the world around me.

Specifically, I'm writing to make the world around me flourish, as it makes me flourish.

I suspect that it may fall short of achieving that, so the least I can do is disclose the ingredients of my writing, and the reader can therefore decide if it's worth the risk of reading.

This same level of scrutiny, is, by the way, what you should be applying before relying on a chatbot for any sort of life advice.

Recent influences

At present, Aeon is the magazine I read most, for its long essays on philosophy, psychology, politics, and other investigations at the corners of knowledge.

If the only effect that this blog has is to get more people to read Aeon, I will be happy.

I also try to tackle some Noema, which is more of the same, but a little more impenetrable in its manifestos.

I'm very happy to still be receiving Nature magazine weekly.

I read the magazine part, not the peer reviewed research.

Do I think it's valuable to listen to scientists' opinions about world affairs?

Absolutely.

These articles are accessible in web format.

I also read comic books.

Always have, and still do.

The "pacifists who punch people" category of superhero comic book seems to be what I gravitate towards, i.e. Superman rip-offs.

Absolute Batman is my current favourite, which removes his trust fund and replaces it with a formal engineering education.

He still keeps all the gadgets, we're just supposed to believe that he built them all himself, using cheap components.

I guess that's actually credible in 2026.

Influences of my influences

Superhero comic books have and always will be written with a political and moral purpose.

Superman was written in the 1930s by two Jewish men as a response to the Nazis' twist on Nietzsche's Übermensch.

Captain America punches Nazis because they're bullies, Batman is anti-guns, Spider-Man uses his radioactive power responsibly, Professor X is Martin Luther King (not Malcolm X, confusingly, that's who Magneto is supposed to be), and the Black Panther is, well, a Black Panther (although apparently that's a coincidence).

The takeaway should be that largely my sense of right and wrong has been shaped by echoes of the American perspective of World War II, and of the civil rights movement, taught to me in the format of the modern hero myths.

If we dig deeper still, do these modern myths carry the same messages as the ancient ones?

How much of Achilles, or Cúchulainn, or Jesus' moral stance remains in the retelling?

Is Superman the complete opposite of the Nazi Übermensch, or the messiah, or just a correction back to Nietzsche's original idea, killing God and ushering in an era of new gods?

What about these magazines I've been raving about?

Aeon promises that all its articles are written by topic experts, mainly academic experts, but it's not a hard requirement.

It therefore speaks with a similar voice as Nature.

Noema, from the Berggruen Institute, on the other hand, is a bit more radical, and a bit more opaque.

In fact, it grabs the power of ideas to change the world, and wields it just like a masked vigilante might, with a sense of responsibility, "shared perspectives between East and West", but without any tangible accountability: a recent billion dollar endowment ensures they're not even influenced by market forces.

(Whether market forces act as an effective form of accountability in general is a separate topic).

Philosophical Underpinning

Underneath it all, I process all information according to a philosophy, clearly a "Western" philosophy, from a lineage that can be traced back to the ancient Greeks (Aristotle, not Plato), enlightenment era thinking that values the autonomous individual as the truth seeker, reductionism, which attracts the engineer in me with the ability to bring anything down into simpler more manageable effects (intelligence is just pattern recognition, emotions are just hormonal responses to different categories of patterns).

I've read some stuff that's been helpful in augmenting my viewpoint, particularly Mona Chollet's "In Defence of Witches" which rather rudely tried to break my belief in science as a trustworthy system for attaining knowledge.

It didn't quite succeed, but it did honestly rebalance my faith a bit more equally between logic and intuition, making me very receptive, for example, to this recent article about the merits of the "unscientific" way in which Ethiopian marathon runners train.

I've also been training myself out of the "intellectual property" concept, as I have no interest in owning an idea, which so often leads to stunting its growth, preferring instead to use the more ancient concept of "inspiration" although it's abundantly clear to me that all inspirations I'm likely to have are worldly, rather than otherworldly in their origin.

Writing

So why should I write?

To help whatever ideas that happen to channel through me to grow.

The risk is that I'll filter out the good and perpetuate the bad, but as long as I focus on flourishing of the sort that allows more than just people like me to flourish.

It's up to you as a reader if that's a risk you're willing to take.

One more Aeon article for the road! ◆
