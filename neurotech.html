Neurotechnology

NeuroRestore's mission is to find a cure for spinal cord injury. The approach is both neuroprosthetic and rehabilitative in nature, reconnecting intention with action through the integration of sensors and stimulators.

Nature · May 2023

Brain-Spine Interface

Restoring natural walking after chronic spinal cord injury

A wireless cortical implant decodes movement intent and drives a spinal stimulator in real time. Intensity modulation lets the user intuitively adapt step size while walking in real-world environments.

Lorach et al., Nature, May 2023 →
Nature Medicine · February 2022

Activity-Dependent Neuromodulation

Stand, walk, cycle, swim — on day one

A new electrode arrangement targets the full dorsal root ensemble rather than the conventional dorsal column. Three participants with complete paralysis recovered functionality such as walking, cycling, swimming and, a function of underappreciated importance: trunk stability.

Rowald et al., Nature Medicine →
Nature Medicine · November 2023

Spinal Neuroprosthesis for Parkinson's Disease

Alleviating freezing of gait in treatment-resistant Parkinson's

A closed-loop spinal implant reproduces the natural muscle-activation pattern of walking. In a patient with 30 years of advanced Parkinson's, it dramatically reduced freezing of gait.

Milekovic et al., Nature Medicine →
Science Robotics · March 2025

Rehabilitation Robotics and Spinal Neuromodulation

Making robot-assisted rehabilitation actually work

Building on the sensor-actuator approach, NeuroRestore's stimulation platform technology was integrated with robotics, offering enhanced rehabilitation capabilities.

Hankov et al., Science Robotics →
Nature Medicine · September 2025

Restoring Blood Pressure After Spinal Cord Injury

Eliminating chronic hypotensive complications

Stimulation of the last three thoracic segments reawakened dormant blood pressure regulating circuitry to alleviate hypotension in 14 tetraplegic participants.

Phillips et al., Nature Medicine →
Preprint · 2026

Digital Bridge for Tetraplegia

ECoG + cervical epidural stimulation

A cervical brain-spine interface decodes up to 8 motor states via a foundation model and restores intentional arm and hand movements in people with chronic incomplete tetraplegia.

Collin, Yu et al., EPFL Infoscience →
