Physics World Breakthrough of the Year 2023

Brain-Spine Interface

Restoring natural walking after chronic spinal cord injury

A cortical implant decodes movement intent wirelessly, in real time, driving a spinal stimulator that reawakens circuits below the injury. The system was deployed in patients' homes — the first time a brain-spine interface operated outside a clinical setting.

Lorach et al., Nature, May 2023 →

Nature Medicine · February 2022

Activity-Dependent Neuromodulation

Stand, walk, cycle, swim — on day one

Epidural stimulation electrodes arranged to target the full ensemble of dorsal roots governing leg and trunk movement, rather than the conventional dorsal column approach. A computational framework guided both surgical electrode placement and stimulation mapping. Three participants with complete sensorimotor paralysis recovered diverse motor function — standing, walking, cycling, swimming, and trunk control — within a single day of optimisation.

Rowald et al., Nature Medicine →
Nature Medicine · November 2023

Spinal Neuroprosthesis for Parkinson's Disease

Alleviating freezing of gait in treatment-resistant Parkinson's

A closed-loop spinal implant targets the lumbosacral dorsal roots in real time, reproducing the natural spatiotemporal pattern of muscle activation during walking. First validated in a non-human primate model, then implanted in a 62-year-old patient with 30 years of advanced Parkinson's disease — improving stride length and symmetry, restoring balance, and dramatically reducing freezing of gait, working alongside existing deep brain stimulation and medication.

Milekovic et al., Nature Medicine →
Science Robotics · March 2025

Rehabilitation Robotics and Spinal Neuromodulation

Making robot-assisted rehabilitation actually work

Paralysis severs the voluntary muscle activation that rehabilitation robotics depend on — patients cannot generate the neural activity needed to benefit from the therapy. This study integrates an implanted spinal neuroprosthesis with commercial rehabilitation robots in a closed loop, delivering well-timed spinal stimulation in sync with robotic movement. Five participants showed immediate and sustained muscle activation, enabling outdoor walking and cycling. The system is designed to be device-agnostic and operable by non-experts.

Hankov et al., Science Robotics →
Nature Medicine · September 2025

Restoring Blood Pressure After Spinal Cord Injury

Eliminating chronic hypotensive complications

Spinal cord injury frequently causes dysautonomia — the autonomic nervous system loses control of blood pressure, triggering dangerous hypotensive episodes that undermine rehabilitation and daily life. This study identified a critical insight: stimulation must target the last three thoracic spinal segments, not the lumbosacral region used for walking. In 14 participants, biomimetic epidural stimulation instantly induced robust pressor responses, durably resolved hypotensive episodes across 1,479 participants studied, and eliminated the need for conservative treatments — restoring independence in daily activities.

Phillips et al., Nature Medicine →
