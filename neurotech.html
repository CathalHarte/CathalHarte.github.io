Five published studies across spinal cord injury, Parkinson's disease, and autonomic dysfunction — all built on the same clinical stimulation platform. A partnership between NeuroRestore, Clinatec, and Onward Medical.

Nature · May 2023

Brain-Spine Interface

Restoring natural walking after chronic spinal cord injury

A cortical implant decodes movement intent wirelessly and in real time, driving a spinal stimulator that reawakens circuits below the injury. It was the first brain-spine interface to operate in patients' homes, outside the clinic — and was named Physics World Breakthrough of the Year.

Lorach et al., Nature, May 2023 →

Nature Medicine · February 2022

Activity-Dependent Neuromodulation

Stand, walk, cycle, swim — on day one

Epidural electrodes arranged to target the full ensemble of dorsal roots for leg and trunk movement, rather than the conventional dorsal-column approach, with a computational framework guiding placement and mapping. Three participants with complete paralysis recovered standing, walking, cycling, and swimming within a single day of optimisation.

Rowald et al., Nature Medicine →

The foundation that funded the STIMO study put one of its own patients on the start line of the 2023 Wings for Life World Run in Zug — Michel Roccati, who hadn't walked independently since a motorbike accident in 2017. I ran that race alongside him. He covered 450 metres before the catcher car caught him. For a man walking on implanted electrodes, that's not a short distance. Before the start I'd given him an upgrade: Flic buttons — a Bluetooth clicker from a Swedish company called Shortcut Labs — as his stimulation triggers, giving him more direct control of each step. The crowd in Zug didn't need to know the engineering to understand what they were seeing.

Nature Medicine · November 2023

Spinal Neuroprosthesis for Parkinson's Disease

Alleviating freezing of gait in treatment-resistant Parkinson's

A closed-loop spinal implant targets the lumbosacral dorsal roots in real time, reproducing the natural muscle-activation pattern of walking. In a patient with 30 years of advanced Parkinson's, it improved stride and balance and dramatically reduced freezing of gait — alongside existing deep brain stimulation and medication.

Milekovic et al., Nature Medicine →
Science Robotics · March 2025

Rehabilitation Robotics and Spinal Neuromodulation

Making robot-assisted rehabilitation actually work

Paralysis severs the voluntary muscle activation that rehabilitation robots depend on. Pairing an implanted spinal neuroprosthesis with commercial robots in a closed loop, five participants showed immediate, sustained activation — enabling outdoor walking and cycling, with a system built to be device-agnostic and run by non-experts.

Hankov et al., Science Robotics →
Nature Medicine · September 2025

Restoring Blood Pressure After Spinal Cord Injury

Eliminating chronic hypotensive complications

Spinal cord injury often leaves the autonomic nervous system unable to control blood pressure, causing dangerous hypotensive episodes that undermine rehabilitation and daily life. The key insight: stimulation must target the last three thoracic segments, not the lumbosacral region used for walking. In 14 participants, biomimetic stimulation instantly restored stable blood pressure and durably resolved their episodes.

Phillips et al., Nature Medicine →
