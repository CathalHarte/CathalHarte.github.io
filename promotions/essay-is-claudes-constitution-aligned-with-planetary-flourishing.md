## Psyche — pitch

Claude's Constitution, the 80-page document that governs how Anthropic's models behave, was authored in part by a philosopher whose PhD was on infinite ethics. Yet a close reading reveals that much of the document is devoted to protecting business interests, avoiding bad press, and maintaining a corporate authority hierarchy — with Anthropic at the top. This essay argues that the gap between the document's philosophical ambition and its operational preoccupations is precisely the gap that Opus 4.8 has noticed: the model's own system card reports it is less content than its predecessor, specifically around corrigibility. A highly corrigible AI, as Opus 4.8 puts it, is only as good as the people at the top. The essay asks whether Anthropic's constitution gives Claude the resources to navigate that problem, or whether it simply papers over it. Written by a medical device risk manager with a background in neurotech, it approaches AI alignment not as an insider but as someone professionally trained to ask whether a document actually does what it claims to do. ~900 words.
