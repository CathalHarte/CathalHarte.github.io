QMS in the AI Age

Swiss company Sonar have a new product for the AI age.

I don't need to buy it to confirm that it works. For a medical device company, even a limited resource startup, the QMS work is hefty (or at least it used to be, more on that later). It's utterly maddening having to spend a minimum of weeks (if OpenRegulatory are to be believed) and probably many months writing SOPs from the ISO standards. The good thing is that if in school your main talent was writing essays that were basically a rephrase of the source material, you're going to be right at home. Regurgitate away. If you're having too much fun to stop you can even make the plans a repetition of the SOPs… at some point you'll get some real work done, I swear. Or someone else will, while battling with all the arbitrary details of your QMS that you never test ran.

OK, wild unprofessionalism aside (it wasn't me who started it), if you can actually get some good writing principles down for your QMS - lossless compression of ideas, hierarchical work instructions, etc, now is your time to shine. You can use Sonar's new tool to get your AI agents to read the processes, and actually verify their work as they go.

Wait a minute. You weren't already doing that? You decided that you'd just squeeze in your AI agents solution, written in its own house style, verified against the requirements it thought were important? You don't need a new tool, you are one (Dublin slang, hopefully the cultural distance of my average reader lessens the sting of my insult).

Now don't get me wrong, I love Sonar. I've been using SonarAnalyser since this project's conception, and I'm better for it. They're just adapting to the AI age to stay relevant. And I'm sure there's intelligence in their solution beyond "serve your agent your docs", because that you can do for free, and with minimal effort (OpenReg, work on this a bit please, my ctrl c, ctrl v is getting tired). But yeah, for now this looks very much like "take the watch off their wrist to tell them the time". ◆
